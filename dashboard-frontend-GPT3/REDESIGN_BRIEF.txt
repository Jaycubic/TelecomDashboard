Dashboard UX/UI Redesign Challenge — Airtel vs Jio

You are being given an existing working dashboard codebase, an Airtel dataset, and a corresponding Jio dataset with the same analytical structure.

Your task is NOT to incrementally improve the existing UI.

Your task is to act as a senior UX/UI designer and product designer and completely rethink the dashboard from first principles.

Imagine you are a highly experienced Apple-level product designer with 10+ years of experience designing polished, data-heavy consumer products. You have been asked to turn this existing technical dashboard into a beautiful, intuitive, premium data product.

OBJECTIVE

Create the strongest dashboard experience you can for a user who wants to understand:

How does Airtel compare with Jio in customer-reported call quality across India?

You have full creative authority over the UX/UI and information architecture.

Do not feel obligated to preserve the existing layout, cards, charts, filters, headings, sidebar, navigation, terminology, or visual hierarchy.

You may completely redesign the interface.

You may remove existing components.

You may replace visualizations.

You may reorganize the entire page.

You may introduce a completely different interaction model.

The ONLY explicit visual requirement is that the geographic map must remain part of the final experience.

Everything else is your decision.

DESIGN RESEARCH

Before redesigning, use web search to research and gather inspiration from high-quality data products, information dashboards, mapping products, Apple interfaces, premium analytics products, editorial data visualization, and other excellent UX/UI references.

Use the web for design inspiration and UX research, not as a source of replacement data.

Do not simply reproduce an existing dashboard you find online.

I want original design thinking informed by excellent examples.

Look at how strong products handle:

- information hierarchy
- comparison
- geographic visualization
- filtering
- progressive disclosure
- typography
- spacing
- interaction
- visual storytelling
- semantic color
- accessibility
- responsive layouts
- data density
- empty/edge states
- tooltips
- micro-interactions
- visual emphasis

You are not being judged on how closely you reproduce any existing design.

You are being judged on the quality of your own design decisions.

IMPORTANT ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT

The existing backend and API layer are already working.

DO NOT modify, replace, break, or redesign the backend/API architecture.

Keep all existing API calls and backend integrations intact.

Your responsibility is primarily the frontend UX/UI and presentation layer.

Use the existing APIs and data correctly.

Do not create fake numbers simply to make the interface look better.

Do not hardcode results that should come from the existing API.

Do not remove functionality merely because it is inconvenient to implement.

You may completely change HOW the functionality is presented and interacted with.

If the current frontend architecture prevents a good design, refactor the frontend as necessary while keeping the backend/API contract intact.

DATA

You will receive:

- the existing project ZIP/codebase
- the Airtel dataset
- the corresponding Jio dataset

The Airtel and Jio datasets represent comparable customer-reported voice/call-quality information.

Use the datasets to understand the available dimensions, relationships, and analytical possibilities.

Do not invent unsupported analytical dimensions.

You should inspect the data before making major visualization decisions.

THE MAP

The geographic map is mandatory.

However, YOU decide how the map should work.

You decide:

- what the map communicates
- what its colors mean
- what is displayed geographically
- how Airtel and Jio are compared
- whether the map uses fills, outlines, overlays, patterns, or another visual treatment
- whether hover/click interactions exist
- how uncertainty or limited data is communicated
- how the map connects to the rest of the dashboard

The map does NOT have to look like the current implementation.

The map only needs to remain as a meaningful geographic component of the product.

DO NOT ANCHOR YOURSELF TO THE EXISTING UI

Treat the current dashboard as an existing technical implementation, not as a design specification.

You are free to completely discard its visual language.

Do not assume that the existing:

- header
- sidebar
- KPI cards
- radar chart
- filter controls
- legends
- labels
- typography
- layout
- colors
- section structure
- chart choices

are good design decisions.

They may all be replaced.

Do not preserve something simply because it already exists.

Ask instead:

"If I were designing this product from zero today, how would I make this information immediately understandable and visually compelling?"

USER EXPERIENCE GOAL

The finished product should be understandable to a normal user without requiring statistical, data-science, or engineering knowledge.

A user should be able to enter the dashboard and quickly understand:

what is being compared -> what the major differences are -> where those differences occur -> what details explain them

Do not optimize the interface for developers.

Do not expose implementation terminology merely because it exists in the dataset or API.

Use human-readable language.

Technical information can exist where useful, but it should not dominate the primary experience.

AESTHETIC DIRECTION

Prioritize:

clarity + elegance + restraint + visual hierarchy + strong typography + meaningful data visualization + excellent interaction design

The result should feel like a finished product, not a collection of components assembled from a dashboard library.

Pay particular attention to:

- spacing
- alignment
- typography
- hierarchy
- density
- contrast
- visual rhythm
- semantic use of color
- consistency
- interaction states
- hover states
- responsive behavior
- accessibility

Avoid visual noise.

Avoid unnecessary decoration.

Avoid "dashboard clichés" unless there is a strong reason for them.

Every major visual element should have a purpose.

COLOR SYSTEM

Do not blindly inherit the current color system.

Think carefully about whether Airtel/Jio colors should represent identity while other colors represent meaning.

Establish a coherent semantic color system appropriate to the information being displayed.

Color should communicate meaning, not merely decorate the interface.

The interface must remain understandable when color is unavailable or difficult to distinguish.

IMPLEMENTATION TASK

After researching and analyzing the existing project:

1. Inspect the complete codebase and understand the current frontend/API architecture.
2. Inspect the provided datasets and understand what can legitimately be shown.
3. Perform your own UX/product analysis.
4. Research relevant design inspiration online.
5. Develop your own information architecture.
6. Completely redesign the dashboard.
7. Implement the redesigned experience in the provided codebase.
8. Preserve the existing backend/API behavior.
9. Make sure the application actually runs.
10. Test the important interactions and states.

Do not stop at producing a concept or wireframe.

Actually implement the design in the provided project.

YOUR DESIGN DECISIONS ARE YOURS

Do not ask me to choose:

- which chart to use
- which filters to use
- whether to keep cards
- whether to keep the sidebar
- which layout to use
- which visualization is best
- how to structure the page
- what belongs above the fold

Make those decisions yourself.

I am deliberately giving you this freedom because I want to evaluate your design judgment.

DELIVERABLE: WORKING DASHBOARD + DESIGN RATIONALE

After implementation, provide a concise but substantive Design Decision Report.

The report should explain the reasoning behind the final product.

Include:

1. DESIGN CONCEPT
What overall experience did you create?
What is the central idea behind the dashboard?

2. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
Explain how you decided what users see first, second, and third.

3. VISUALIZATION DECISIONS
For every major visualization, explain:
- why you chose it
- what question it answers
- why it is appropriate for this data
- what alternatives you considered or rejected

4. MAP DESIGN
Explain:
- what the map communicates
- why you represented the geographic comparison that way
- how the user interacts with it
- how you handle limited/uncertain data

5. FILTERS AND INTERACTIONS
Explain:
- which filters you kept
- which you removed
- why
- how the interaction model works

6. COLOR SYSTEM
Explain:
- operator identity
- semantic meaning
- positive/negative/neutral states
- accessibility considerations

7. TYPOGRAPHY AND VISUAL HIERARCHY
Explain how typography, spacing, scale, and grouping guide the user's attention.

8. TECHNICAL DECISIONS
Explain any significant frontend restructuring while confirming that the existing backend/API contracts were preserved.

9. UX IMPROVEMENTS
Explain how the redesign reduces cognitive load and makes the data easier to understand.

10. TRADE-OFFS
Be honest about important compromises or limitations in the final design.

FINAL PRINCIPLE

Do not try to make the existing dashboard "slightly better."

REIMAGINE IT.

I am intentionally giving you freedom to make bold design decisions.

The final result will be evaluated as a PRODUCT EXPERIENCE, not as a code exercise.

The goal is not to prove that you can add more charts.

The goal is to demonstrate that you can take a complex comparative dataset and turn it into an exceptionally clear, aesthetically strong, intuitive, and usable interface.

YOU ARE THE DESIGNER. MAKE THE CALLS.
